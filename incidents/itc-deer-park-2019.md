## What Happened

A circulation pump connected to Tank 80-8, an 80,000-barrel aboveground storage tank holding a naphtha-butane blend, failed due to a bearing failure. The pump kept running for roughly 30 minutes after the failure, releasing flammable product that formed a vapor cloud before igniting near the tank's piping manifold. ITC had no way to remotely isolate the leak — operators would have needed to manually close valves at the source, but the fire made that area impossible to reach for both company personnel and emergency responders. The fire spread to 14 neighboring tanks in the same containment area and burned for three days before being extinguished. No injuries or fatalities resulted, but the incident caused over $150 million in property damage, forced repeated shelter-in-place orders across Deer Park over benzene air quality concerns, closed a 7-mile stretch of the Houston Ship Channel, and released hundreds of thousands of barrels of hydrocarbon products and contaminated firefighting water into nearby waterways.

## Root Causes

- **Pump mechanical integrity failure with no early warning:** control room data showed changing pump pressures and tank flow beginning early that morning, but nothing was configured to trigger an alarm on those changes.
- **A 2014 safety recommendation was never implemented:** a hazard review team had specifically recommended adding flammable gas detection near this tank five years earlier — ITC never implemented it and never documented why.
- **No remotely operated emergency isolation valves:** stopping the release required manually closing valves at a location that became inaccessible once the fire started, removing the one intervention that could have limited the incident's scale.
- **No formal mechanical integrity procedure for this equipment:** CSB found no established program for maintaining the tank and its associated pump and piping.
- **A regulatory gap left this equipment outside standard safety requirements:** CSB found some of the tanks involved weren't covered by OSHA or EPA process safety rules, so ITC wasn't legally required to implement safeguards that could have prevented this.

## Lessons Learned

1. A safety recommendation that's identified but never implemented — and never even documented as rejected — offers no protection at all; this is the same pattern seen elsewhere in this database (TPC Port Neches, PCA DeRidder), where a known fix sat unactioned for years before an incident proved it was needed.
2. Remote emergency isolation capability matters most exactly when you can't afford to wait — a fire that makes manual valves unreachable is the scenario remote isolation exists for, not an edge case to plan around later.
3. Instrumentation changes (like shifting pump pressure or flow) need to be configured to actually alert someone — data quietly existing in a control system isn't the same as a functioning early-warning system.
4. Regulatory coverage gaps are a real, recurring theme across this database, not a one-off technicality — a facility falling outside PSM or RMP requirements is not thereby exempt from the underlying hazard, and voluntary safeguards matter most precisely where mandatory ones don't reach.
