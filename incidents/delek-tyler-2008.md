## What Happened

On October 20, 2008, a line ruptured in the saturated gas unit at Delek Refining Ltd.'s facility in Tyler, Texas, triggering an explosion and fire. Two workers, James Hammett, 52, and James Dale Sharman, 49, both from Tyler, were killed. Three other workers were injured in the incident. The saturated gas unit processes lighter hydrocarbon fractions from the refining process, and a rupture in this kind of equipment releases pressurized, flammable gas capable of igniting rapidly once released. OSHA's subsequent investigation examined the mechanical failure that led to the line rupture as part of its broader review of process safety conditions at the refinery.

## Root Causes

- **A line in the saturated gas unit ruptured:** the direct cause of the incident was a mechanical failure of process piping carrying hydrocarbon gas, releasing pressurized flammable material into the surrounding area.
- **The released gas ignited, causing an explosion and fire:** once the line failed, the escaping hydrocarbon gas found an ignition source, and the resulting explosion and fire caused the fatal and injurious consequences.
- **The specific mechanical integrity failure mode requires further primary-source detail:** while OSHA investigated this incident, publicly available secondary sources describing this event do not detail the exact corrosion, fatigue, or other mechanism behind the line's failure.

## Lessons Learned

1. Process piping carrying pressurized hydrocarbon gas needs a mechanical integrity program with inspection intervals and criteria matched to the specific degradation mechanisms that piping is exposed to, given how quickly a rupture in this kind of service can escalate to a fatal explosion.
2. Saturated gas units and similar processing equipment handling lighter, more volatile hydrocarbon fractions deserve particular attention in a facility's mechanical integrity program, since the material's volatility means a rupture can transition to ignition very quickly.
3. This incident is one of many documented refinery fatalities from mechanical piping failures across this database, underscoring that corrosion and fatigue-related pipe failures remain a persistent, recurring root cause across the industry, not a rare or unusual event.
4. When only limited public detail exists about a fatal incident's specific technical cause, that itself is worth noting. Full transparency about root cause findings, even for incidents that don't receive the same level of investigative coverage as larger disasters, helps the broader industry learn from every fatality, not just the most widely publicized ones.
