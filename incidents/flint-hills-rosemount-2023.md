## What Happened

At approximately 7:45 a.m. on April 8, 2023, hydrogen sulfide and sulfur dioxide were released from the sulfur recovery unit at the Flint Hills Resources Pine Bend Refinery in Rosemount, Minnesota, resulting in approximately $2.235 million in property damage. The company's investigation determined the release resulted from a ruptured tube within the waste heat boiler of the sulfur recovery reaction furnace. The tube failed due to sulfidation corrosion, also known as high-temperature sulfidic corrosion, a well-documented damage mechanism in which sulfur compounds react with iron-containing materials under certain temperature conditions, gradually thinning the metal over time. Flint Hills Resources' investigation found that fouling from hydrocarbon solids had formed on the outside of some tube wall surfaces, raising the local tube wall temperature and accelerating metal loss from corrosion in those specific areas. The investigation further traced this hydrocarbon fouling to contamination in the boiler feedwater itself, indicating the feedwater quality was not being effectively monitored, which allowed the contamination to persist undetected. The release totaled approximately 68 pounds of hydrogen sulfide and 999 pounds of sulfur dioxide. Following the incident, Flint Hills Resources implemented continuous in-line boiler feedwater monitoring using analyzers that strip and quantitatively measure hydrocarbon content in near-real time, allowing the company to detect when feedwater quality falls outside industry-recommended standards and take corrective action promptly.

## Root Causes

- **Sulfidation corrosion thinned a waste heat boiler tube until it ruptured:** this well-known damage mechanism, driven by sulfur compounds reacting with the tube's iron-containing material, progressively weakened the tube wall over time.
- **Hydrocarbon fouling on the tube's exterior raised local wall temperature:** solids that accumulated on parts of the tube surface acted as insulation, increasing the metal's temperature in those spots and accelerating the corrosion rate specifically where the fouling had formed.
- **Boiler feedwater contamination with hydrocarbons went undetected:** the fouling that ultimately drove localized overheating originated from hydrocarbon contamination in the feedwater supply, a condition the facility wasn't actively monitoring for.
- **No real-time feedwater quality monitoring existed before the incident:** without continuous analysis capable of detecting hydrocarbon presence, the facility had no way to catch this contamination early enough to prevent it from contributing to tube damage over time.

## Lessons Learned

1. Sulfidation corrosion is a recognized, predictable damage mechanism in sulfur-containing hydrocarbon processes, and boiler and heat exchanger tubes in this service need inspection programs specifically designed around this known failure mode, not generic wall-thickness checks alone.
2. Fouling on heat transfer surfaces doesn't just reduce efficiency; it can directly accelerate corrosion by locally raising metal temperature, meaning fouling control is a mechanical integrity issue as much as an operational one.
3. Boiler feedwater quality needs continuous, real-time monitoring for contaminants like hydrocarbons, not periodic or reactive testing, since undetected contamination can silently drive localized equipment damage over an extended period before a failure occurs.
4. When an incident's root cause traces back to feedwater or utility quality issues, the corrective action needs to address that upstream contamination source directly, as Flint Hills Resources did by implementing in-line analyzers, rather than only reinforcing inspection of the equipment that ultimately failed.
