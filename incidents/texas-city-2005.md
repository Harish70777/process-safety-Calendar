## What Happened

During startup of the isomerization (ISOM) unit, operators overfilled a distillation tower (the raffinate splitter) with flammable liquid due to a faulty level indicator and a poorly executed startup procedure. The excess liquid overflowed into a blowdown drum and an outdated atmospheric vent stack, releasing a dense hydrocarbon vapor cloud that found an ignition source and exploded. All fatalities occurred in or around contractor trailers positioned as close as 121 feet from the process unit — trailers housing turnaround workers, not the unit being restarted.

**Result:** 15 deaths, 180 injuries, ~$200 million in property damage, ~$2.5 billion in total liabilities.

## Root Causes

- **Instrumentation failure & procedural gaps:** the tower's level indicator was unreliable, and there were no procedures for calibrating, inspecting, or maintaining several of the instruments later identified as contributing causes.
- **Facility siting failure:** occupied trailers were sited within a hazardous process area with a documented history of releases and fires.
- **Chronic underinvestment in maintenance:** years of budget cuts to maintenance, training, and safety staffing followed cost-cutting mandates after the Amoco–BP merger.
- **Organizational/cultural failure:** the CSB concluded this was an accident with organizational causes embedded in the refinery's culture — the site's own safety plan had warned earlier that year it would likely "kill someone in the next 12–18 months."

## Lessons Learned

1. Audit occupied-building siting against API RP 752/753 — this incident is the reason RP 752 was revised.
2. Startup and shutdown are your highest-risk operating modes; confirm your PSSR process actually verifies instrumentation is functional, not just "was working before."
3. Track near-miss and incident frequency as a leading indicator, not a lagging embarrassment.
4. Maintenance budget cuts should trigger a formal risk review, not just fall out of the annual budgeting cycle.
