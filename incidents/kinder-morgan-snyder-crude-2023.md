## What Happened

Shortly before 1:00 p.m. on February 11, 2023, a pump failure released hydrocarbons, primarily crude oil, along with nitrogen oxide at a Kinder Morgan crude oil production facility in Snyder, Texas. The released crude oil ignited, causing a fire that damaged nearby equipment and led to additional material releases. Kinder Morgan estimated the property damage at approximately $1.5 million. The company's investigation found the pump had experienced excessive wear and tear, and that its bearing lock nut, the component holding the bearings in place, had become loose. This loose lock nut allowed the pump shaft to shift and contact other internal pump components, causing damage that ultimately released crude oil from the pump into the atmosphere. Heat generated by metal-to-metal contact in the pump's seal area was identified as the likely ignition source for the released material. During the incident, more than 1,500 barrels of hydrocarbons, primarily crude oil, and more than 1,200 pounds of nitrogen oxide were released.

## Root Causes

- **The pump experienced excessive wear and tear:** the underlying mechanical condition that set this failure in motion was degradation of the pump beyond what its design and maintenance program had accounted for.
- **A bearing lock nut became loose:** this specific component failure allowed the pump shaft to move out of its intended position, the direct mechanical trigger for the subsequent damage.
- **Shaft movement caused internal components to contact each other:** once the shaft could shift, it made contact with other pump parts, damaging the pump and creating the release path for crude oil.
- **Metal-to-metal contact generated enough heat to serve as an ignition source:** the friction from the damaged components rubbing together likely provided the spark or heat needed to ignite the released hydrocarbon material.

## Lessons Learned

1. Pump wear and mechanical condition need monitoring and preventive maintenance sufficient to catch degradation before it progresses to component failure, particularly for pumps handling flammable material where a failure carries fire risk in addition to the release itself.
2. Fastener integrity for critical rotating equipment components, like bearing lock nuts, needs verification as part of routine mechanical integrity checks, since a single loosened fastener was sufficient to initiate this entire failure sequence.
3. Rotating equipment failures involving shaft movement or component contact carry an inherent ignition risk from friction-generated heat, meaning any pump handling flammable liquids needs monitoring capable of detecting abnormal vibration or shaft position before metal-to-metal contact actually occurs.
4. Vibration monitoring and predictive maintenance programs for pumps in flammable liquid service provide real value specifically because they can detect the kind of progressive wear that preceded this failure well before it reaches the point of component contact and ignition.
