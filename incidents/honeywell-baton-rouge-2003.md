## What Happened

Over a 24-day span in the summer of 2003, the Honeywell International chemical plant in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, experienced three separate hazardous chemical releases that CSB ultimately investigated together, finding common underlying management deficiencies behind all three. On July 20, a release of chlorine gas injured seven plant workers and prompted a shelter-in-place advisory for residents within a half-mile radius. Nine days later, on July 29, a 1-ton cylinder at the same plant released its contents to the atmosphere; a plant worker was fatally exposed to antimony pentachloride, a highly corrosive and toxic compound, during that release. Then on August 13, two additional workers were exposed to hydrogen fluoride, with one requiring hospitalization. CSB's investigation into all three incidents identified overlapping deficiencies in the facility's hazard analysis and management systems, finding that existing hazard reviews had not ensured all relevant equipment was adequately assessed for the risks it actually posed.

## Root Causes

- **Hazard analyses did not ensure review of all relevant equipment:** CSB found the facility's process hazard analyses had gaps in equipment coverage, meaning some of the equipment involved in these releases had not received the kind of thorough hazard review that could have identified developing risks.
- **A pattern of three related incidents within 24 days pointed to systemic issues:** rather than being three unconnected events, CSB's joint investigation found common management system deficiencies ran through all three releases, indicating the underlying gaps hadn't been addressed even after the first incident occurred.
- **A cylinder valve or fitting failed during a material release:** the fatal July 29 incident involved an uncontrolled release from a 1-ton cylinder, exposing a worker to antimony pentachloride during what should have been a controlled transfer or handling operation.
- **Inadequate management of change contributed across the incidents:** CSB's overall analysis found that changes to equipment or procedures at the facility had not consistently gone through the kind of review needed to catch the hazards that ultimately materialized in these releases.

## Lessons Learned

1. When a facility experiences a serious chemical release, the response needs to include a genuine review of whether the same underlying management gaps could affect other equipment or processes on site. This facility had two more significant releases in the following three weeks after the first.
2. Hazard analyses need to comprehensively cover all process equipment handling hazardous materials, not leave gaps that allow some equipment to operate without the same level of scrutiny applied elsewhere in the facility.
3. Cylinder and container handling for highly toxic, corrosive materials like antimony pentachloride needs verified, redundant controls, given that a single valve or fitting failure during transfer can directly and immediately expose a worker to a fatal hazard.
4. A pattern of multiple incidents at the same facility within a short time window is a strong signal that corrective action after the first event wasn't sufficient. CSB's decision to investigate all three releases jointly reflects exactly this kind of pattern recognition, and facilities experiencing repeat incidents should apply the same scrutiny to their own history.
