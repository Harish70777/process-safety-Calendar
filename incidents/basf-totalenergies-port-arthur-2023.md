## What Happened

At 11:53 a.m. on September 5, 2023, a distillation tower collapsed at the BASF TOTALEnergies Petrochemical facility in Port Arthur, Texas, following an internal fire, causing approximately $194 million in property damage. The company had shut down its Pyrolysis Gasoline unit, including the affected extractive distillation tower, a few weeks earlier and followed its procedure to remove solvent and hydrocarbons in preparation for maintenance. On the day of the incident, a maintenance crew began opening manways to prepare the tower for entry. By 9:30 a.m., temperatures inside the tower's stainless steel structured packing beds began rising; by 10:44 a.m., smoke and flames were visible, and an emergency alarm sounded. Personnel evacuated after adding nitrogen and attempting to stop air from entering the tower, but at 11:53 a.m. the tower folded over and collapsed. The company's investigation traced the fire to a chain of events beginning about a month before the shutdown, when equipment leaks elsewhere in the process allowed water to enter the extractive distillation unit, causing internal corrosion that removed iron from the tower's metal walls. This iron combined with hydrogen sulfide normally present in the process to form iron sulfide, a pyrophoric material that ignites spontaneously on contact with air. When the manways were opened for maintenance entry, oxygen entered the tower and triggered an exothermic iron sulfide oxidation reaction, generating localized heat between 1,300 and 1,800 degrees Fahrenheit within the packing, weakening the tower's structure until it collapsed. Notably, a 2016 incident at the same facility had already revealed the potential for iron sulfide to accumulate inside this exact tower, but that knowledge had not been effectively carried forward to the operations personnel involved in 2023.

## Root Causes

- **Water ingress from unrelated equipment leaks caused internal corrosion:** roughly a month before the incident, leaks elsewhere in the process allowed water into the extractive distillation unit, corroding the tower's internal metal surfaces and releasing iron.
- **Iron combined with hydrogen sulfide to form pyrophoric iron sulfide inside the tower:** this reactive material accumulated within the structured packing, creating conditions for spontaneous ignition once exposed to air.
- **Opening the manways for maintenance introduced the oxygen that triggered ignition:** the standard preparation step for tower entry, opening access points, was exactly the action that allowed air to reach the pyrophoric material and start the exothermic reaction.
- **Knowledge from a 2016 incident at the same tower was not effectively transferred to current personnel:** the facility had already learned that iron sulfide could accumulate in this specific tower, but that institutional knowledge did not reach the operations team preparing for entry in 2023, preventing them from adjusting their solvent and hydrocarbon removal procedure to account for the known risk.

## Lessons Learned

1. Water ingress into a hydrocarbon process unit needs treatment as a potential precursor to internal corrosion and pyrophoric material formation, not just a leak to be repaired, especially in units known to contain hydrogen sulfide.
2. Opening a vessel for maintenance entry after any period of unusual process conditions (like a water leak) needs a hazard reassessment specific to what might have changed inside, rather than following the standard procedure unmodified.
3. Institutional knowledge from a prior incident at a specific piece of equipment needs a durable transfer mechanism, documentation embedded in the actual maintenance procedure for that equipment, not reliance on personnel memory, especially across the years and staff turnover that separated this facility's 2016 finding from its 2023 recurrence.
4. Pyrophoric material formation is a well-documented hazard in hydrogen-sulfide-containing hydrocarbon processes, and any facility with this process chemistry should maintain active awareness of where such material could be accumulating, treating a prior finding at a specific vessel as a permanent flag on that equipment's maintenance procedures going forward.
