Anniversary: December 13, 1994

Terra Industries Port Neal Ammonium Nitrate Explosion

Sergeant Bluff, IA, USA · 4 fatalities, 18 injuries
Operating ProceduresTrainingMechanical Integrity

What Happened

At approximately 6:06 a.m. on December 13, 1994, two massive detonations occurred in the ammonium nitrate plant at Terra International's Port Neal Complex near Sioux City, Iowa. Investigators determined the explosion originated in a vessel called the neutralizer, where nitric acid is combined with ammonia to produce ammonium nitrate solution, and that this initial explosion triggered a second detonation in a connected rundown tank virtually instantaneously. The explosion occurred after the plant had been shut down with ammonium nitrate solution still left inside several process vessels. Investigators found the neutralizer and rundown tank had developed strongly acidic conditions, contrary to the near-neutral conditions the process was meant to maintain, and that 200 psig steam had been applied to the neutralizer's nitric acid spargers for a prolonged period. These conditions combined to drive a thermal decomposition reaction in the ammonium nitrate solution that accelerated into a deflagration. The blast leveled roughly half the facility, was felt as far as Vermillion, South Dakota, and Norfolk, Nebraska, and blew out windows and interrupted power at a nearby power plant. A ruptured storage tank released an ammonia cloud that spread roughly 15 miles and forced the evacuation of an estimated 2,500 people across two states. Four employees were killed, and 18 others were injured, some by falling debris and blast impact, others from ammonia inhalation. Only 30 of the plant's 119 employees were on site at the time, since the explosion occurred during an early morning shift.

Root Causes

  • Strongly acidic conditions developed in the neutralizer and rundown tank: the process vessels, meant to operate under near-neutral pH conditions, had instead become strongly acidic, a condition that significantly increases the risk of ammonium nitrate solution undergoing dangerous thermal decomposition.
  • Steam was applied to the neutralizer for a prolonged period: continued application of 200 psig steam to the vessel's nitric acid spargers contributed to the buildup of the conditions that ultimately triggered decomposition.
  • The process vessels were left charged with material and unmonitored after shutdown: ammonium nitrate solution remained inside the neutralizer and rundown tank following shutdown without adequate ongoing monitoring to detect the developing hazardous conditions.
  • No written safe operating procedures existed for the ammonium nitrate unit: the EPA's investigation found most operating procedures used in the plant were never formally written down, a gap that made proper training, procedure enforcement, and ongoing evaluation of the procedures for weaknesses all significantly more difficult.

Lessons Learned

  1. Process vessels left charged with reactive material after a shutdown need active, ongoing monitoring, not the assumption that a shutdown state is inherently a safe state. Conditions capable of triggering thermal decomposition developed specifically during this unmonitored period.
  2. pH conditions in ammonium nitrate production need continuous verification against their designed near-neutral range, since a drift toward strongly acidic conditions, as happened here, materially increases the risk of dangerous decomposition.
  3. Written, formal operating procedures aren't a bureaucratic formality. The EPA's investigation specifically found their absence made training, procedure enforcement, and identifying operational weaknesses all significantly harder, a gap that runs through the sequence of conditions leading to this explosion.
  4. Facilities producing or storing large quantities of ammonium nitrate need engineering controls and monitoring specifically designed around this chemical's well-documented decomposition and explosion risks, a hazard that recurs across the historical record for this chemical, including the AZF Toulouse and West Fertilizer incidents also documented in this database.
