Wynnewood Refinery Boiler Explosion

Wynnewood, OK, USA · 2 fatalities, 0 injuries
Operating ProceduresMechanical IntegrityTraining

What Happened

The Wynnewood Refining Company facility was in the middle of a scheduled turnaround, with much of the plant temporarily shut down for maintenance. As operators worked to restart the refinery's Wickes boiler, which had been taken offline during the turnaround, excess natural gas accumulated inside the boiler instead of being properly purged. The boiler exploded during the restart, at approximately 6:30 p.m. Two employees, Russell Mann, 45, and Billy Smith, 34, were fatally injured. Following the incident, OSHA's investigation found the company had failed to implement several of its own written safety policies and procedures, and the facility was placed in OSHA's Severe Violator Enforcement Program. Twelve serious citations were issued, several characterized as repeat violations tied to the same equipment. Wynnewood contested the citations; both an administrative law judge and, later, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Tenth Circuit ultimately upheld OSHA's findings.

Root Causes

  • Natural gas accumulated in the boiler before restart: rather than being fully purged of residual gas before startup, the Wickes boiler retained an unsafe accumulation that ignited during the restart sequence.
  • An improper restart procedure: OSHA's investigation found the boiler was brought back online in a way that didn't follow required Process Safety Management procedures for this exact scenario, a boiler being restarted after being taken offline during a turnaround.
  • Repeat violations of the same safety requirements: several of the citations issued were classified as repeat violations, indicating this wasn't the first time gaps in the same safety systems had been identified.
  • Inadequate hazard recognition for boiler restart operations: the facility's own internal practices did not adequately address the specific hazards of bringing this equipment back into service, despite this being a recurring operational task.

Lessons Learned

  1. Any boiler or vessel being restarted after being taken offline needs a documented, verified purge procedure to confirm no unsafe accumulation of combustible gas remains before ignition sources are reintroduced.
  2. Turnaround periods, when multiple pieces of equipment are simultaneously shut down and restarted, deserve heightened procedural discipline, not less, since the sequence of bringing systems back online safely is itself a distinct hazard scenario from steady-state operation.
  3. Repeat citations for the same underlying safety gap are a clear signal that corrective action after a prior finding wasn't actually effective, tracking whether a fix genuinely closed the gap matters as much as identifying the gap in the first place.
  4. A facility's own written safety procedures only provide protection if they're actually followed during real operations. OSHA's investigation found a gap between what Wynnewood's procedures said and what actually happened during this restart, a pattern that shows up repeatedly across incidents in this database.
