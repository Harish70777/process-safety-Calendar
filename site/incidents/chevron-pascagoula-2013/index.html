Anniversary: November 15, 2013

Chevron Pascagoula Refinery Furnace Explosion

Pascagoula, MS, USA · 1 fatalities, 0 injuries
Operating ProceduresTrainingManagement Of Change

What Happened

A power outage at the Chevron Pascagoula Refinery forced an emergency shutdown of the Cracking II processing unit. As workers relit a series of burners in the unit's furnace to bring the plant back online, the furnace exploded at approximately 2:00 a.m. on November 15, 2013. A dozen operators were positioned outside the furnace at the time; Tonya Graddy, a 46-year-old operator with five years at the refinery, was fatally injured in the blast and the smaller fires that followed. The explosion was contained to the Cracking II unit, and the rest of the roughly 330,000-barrel-per-day refinery, the largest wholly owned Chevron refinery in the country, continued operating throughout the incident. OSHA's subsequent investigation found that proper furnace purging procedures had not been performed on the unit before the restart.

Root Causes

  • A power outage forced an emergency shutdown that led directly into a hazardous restart: the sequence from unplanned power loss to emergency shutdown to furnace relighting created exactly the kind of abnormal, time-pressured restart condition that has caused fatal incidents at other facilities in this database (Wynnewood Refinery).
  • The furnace was not properly purged before burners were relit: OSHA's investigation specifically found that required purging procedures, meant to clear the furnace of any residual combustible gas before reintroducing an ignition source, were not performed.
  • Inadequate documentation of hazard training: OSHA cited Chevron for not providing clear documentation that every employee involved understood the hazardous conditions associated with this restart process.
  • Management of change procedures did not address safety and health impact: OSHA found that substitute management of change procedures used at the facility did not adequately evaluate how changes affected safety and health outcomes.

Lessons Learned

  1. Any furnace or boiler restart following an unplanned power outage needs a verified purge procedure completed before burners are relit, regardless of the operational pressure to bring the unit back online quickly. This exact failure pattern, an unplanned outage followed by an improperly purged restart, has caused fatalities at more than one facility.
  2. Training documentation needs to be clear and verifiable, not just assumed. OSHA's finding that Chevron couldn't clearly document employee understanding of the hazardous restart conditions points to a real gap between training that happened and training that could be confirmed.
  3. Management of change procedures need to explicitly evaluate safety and health impact as a required element, not an optional consideration folded into a broader operational review.
  4. A facility's history of prior incidents deserves ongoing attention. Pascagoula had experienced earlier serious incidents, including a 2007 fire, and OSHA cited related process safety management and training gaps again here, a pattern worth treating as a signal that corrective action from earlier events hadn't fully closed the underlying gaps.
