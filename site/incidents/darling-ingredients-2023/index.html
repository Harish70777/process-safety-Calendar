Darling Ingredients Aluminum Chloride Tank Rupture

Wadesboro, NC, USA · 1 fatalities, 0 injuries
Process Hazard AnalysisMechanical Integrity

What Happened

An explosion occurred in an ancillary building at a Darling Ingredients facility that repurposes animal byproducts, tied to a tank containing aluminum chloride solution. Aluminum chloride solution is inherently unstable and reacts violently, potentially explosively, with water, a hazard the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration explicitly documents. First responders were called to the scene after smoke was seen rising from the building. One worker died in the incident; the company stated the plant was shut down and an investigation was underway, with no threat identified to the surrounding public. This incident was captured in CSB's Incident Reports summary series (compiled from mandatory reports under the agency's Accidental Release Reporting Rule) rather than as a full standalone deep-dive investigation, so the level of public root-cause detail available is more limited than for CSB's fully investigated cases elsewhere in this database.

Root Causes

  • A water-reactive chemical stored or handled in a manner that allowed contact with water: aluminum chloride solution's well-documented tendency to react violently or explosively with water is the central hazard mechanism identified by emergency responders at the scene.
  • Confined space amplifying consequence: the reaction and explosion occurred inside a building, an environment aluminum chloride's known hazard profile specifically flags as elevating explosion risk when the chemical is present near water in enclosed areas.

Lessons Learned

  1. Water-reactive chemicals (aluminum chloride and similar compounds) require storage and handling procedures that positively exclude water contact, this includes protection from routine sources like sprinkler systems, condensation, roof leaks, or nearby wash-down operations, not just obvious bulk water exposure.
  2. Confined or enclosed spaces housing water-reactive materials carry elevated consequence potential if a reaction does occur, facility design should account for this when siting tanks containing such chemicals, favoring open or well-ventilated areas where feasible.
  3. This entry is a reminder that not every serious incident receives the same depth of public investigation, CSB's summary-level incident reporting still surfaces real, current hazards worth tracking, even when a full root-cause investigation isn't available. Readers researching this incident further should look for updates from the North Carolina Department of Labor, which opened its own investigation.
