Concept Sciences Hydroxylamine Explosion

Hanover Township, PA, USA · 5 fatalities, 14 injuries
Process Hazard AnalysisFacility SitingProcess Safety Information

What Happened

Concept Sciences was attempting its first large-scale commercial batch of hydroxylamine (HA), a chemical used in semiconductor manufacturing, at its facility in Hanover Township, Pennsylvania. Employees were distilling an aqueous solution of hydroxylamine and potassium sulfate in a process vessel. After the distillation process was shut down for the day, hydroxylamine remaining in the tank and its associated piping, now at a higher concentration than during active processing and still at an elevated temperature, explosively decomposed. The blast, which registered 0.7 on a seismograph five miles away, destroyed the production building entirely and damaged at least 11 other buildings in the surrounding business park, including a business located immediately next door. The explosion produced a white cloud that carried chemical residue as far as two miles away and could be seen for seven miles. Four Concept Sciences employees were killed, along with a manager at the adjacent business who died from a head injury caused by flying debris. Fourteen people were injured, including two Concept Sciences employees buried beneath debris and several firefighters, some of whom were volunteers who responded to the scene, one of the deceased employees was himself a member of an area volunteer fire company.

Root Causes

  • The explosive decomposition hazard of concentrated hydroxylamine was not adequately recognized: hydroxylamine solutions become significantly more hazardous at higher concentrations and temperatures than the diluted, stabilized solutions generally considered safe for storage, a distinction the facility's process design did not adequately account for.
  • Elevated concentration and temperature developed after the process was shut down: rather than remaining in a stable state, the hydroxylamine left in the tank and piping was in a condition, more concentrated and still hot, that CSB's investigation found was consistent with conditions known to trigger explosive decomposition.
  • This was the company's first attempt at this scale: Concept Sciences had not previously run a production batch of this size, meaning the specific hazards of operating at this scale had not been proven out through prior safe operation.
  • Facility siting placed occupied buildings close to a hazardous process: the explosion's severity was compounded by how close other occupied businesses sat to a facility handling a chemical capable of this kind of explosive decomposition, contributing directly to the fatality outside Concept Sciences' own workforce.

Lessons Learned

  1. Chemicals that are broadly considered stable in common commercial forms, like dilute stabilized hydroxylamine solutions, can become significantly more hazardous at higher concentrations and temperatures. Process design needs to explicitly account for how a chemical's hazard profile changes across the full range of conditions it will actually experience during processing, not just its properties in its most commonly encountered form.
  2. A shutdown or end-of-process state isn't automatically a safe state. This incident's fatal decomposition happened after distillation had already been shut down for the day, when the material was assumed to be settling into an inert condition rather than approaching an explosive one.
  3. The first attempt at a new production scale carries meaningfully elevated risk, since the process hasn't yet been proven safe under those specific conditions. This deserves corresponding caution and hazard review beyond what a routine, previously-validated batch would require.
  4. Facility siting decisions need to account for the worst credible consequence of a hazardous process, including its potential to affect neighboring, unrelated businesses. This incident's fatalities extended beyond the company's own workforce specifically because of how close other occupied buildings sat to the hazard.
